Newspapers grew in every province, with The Hindu in Madras and the Amrita Bazar Patrika from Bengal becoming famous names. Print opened a space for women: Rashsundari Devi learnt to read in secret and published her autobiography Amar Jiban in 1876, the first by an Indian woman; Tarabai Shinde's Stripurush Tulana (1882) compared women and men to attack patriarchy; Pandita Ramabai wrote about the lives of upper-caste Hindu women; and Begum Rokeya Sakhawat Hossein argued for girls' education. From the 1870s Hindi printing grew in north India, and by the early twentieth century journals for women, often edited and run by women themselves, circulated in the bazaars and homes.
